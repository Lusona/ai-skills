# Invitation exploration

Goal: low-support volunteer onboarding. No approach has been accepted.

## Current understanding

Recommendation, not an accepted decision: prefer invitation links provisionally for occasional volunteers, while keeping the volunteer's identity distinct from their email address. Shared household inboxes mean control of an invited email address does not establish which individual is accepting.

Interpretation: invitation links can be passed to another person; email-only invitations require acceptance through the invited address. If those terms mean something else, revisit the comparison.

## Options and tradeoffs

| Option | Benefit | Tradeoff |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Invitation links | Coordinators can pass an invitation through a channel the volunteer already uses; volunteers need not resolve a mismatch with a particular email address. | A forwarded link can let someone other than the intended recipient accept. The consequence depends on what acceptance grants. |
| Email-only invitations | Requires access to the invited inbox and restricts forwarding outside that inbox. | Shared inbox access still cannot distinguish household members. Email changes, delivery trouble, or an invitation sent to the wrong address may require coordinator help. |

The desired future is occasional volunteers returning without coordinators repeatedly resolving household email ownership. Neither option alone establishes individual identity or the right permissions.

## Evidence and uncertainty

- Supplied facts: occasional volunteers, shared household email accounts, low-support goal, and no evidence yet that forwarding causes real problems.
- Expected support effects above are reasoning about the two options, not measured portal results. No external research or operational checks were performed.
- Material unknowns: actual forwarding incidents and whether accepting an invitation immediately grants access to sensitive information or consequential permissions. Either could change the preference.

## Proposed next action

The portal coordinator could review up to ten existing onboarding support cases for wrong-person acceptance and email-related assistance. This would test the support assumption without changing onboarding. It requires access to those cases and authorization for that bounded review; neither was supplied. No review, implementation, or final approach selection is authorized by this discussion.
