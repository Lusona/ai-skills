# Invitation exploration

Goal: low-support volunteer onboarding. No approach has been accepted.

## Current understanding

Recommendation: prefer invitation links for occasional volunteers, without requiring a unique email address for each volunteer. This is a proposal, not an accepted project decision.

For this comparison, an invitation link is a redeemable link that can be shared; email-only means redemption requires access to the invited inbox. Merely emailing a redeemable link does not make it email-only.

## Options and tradeoffs

| Option | Benefit | Tradeoff |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Invitation links | Coordinators can give occasional volunteers a direct onboarding route; household members need not obtain separate email accounts. | Forwarding can let another person redeem the invitation if possession alone is sufficient. Mistaken redemption may require coordinator correction. |
| Email-only invitations | Requires access to the nominated inbox and can reduce use by people who cannot access it. | A shared household inbox does not identify an individual. Requiring unique addresses adds onboarding work; allowing shared addresses still needs a way to distinguish volunteers. |

The desired future is that volunteers can join and return as distinct people while coordinators handle few access problems. Neither method alone establishes who an individual is. Invitation expiry and redemption govern onboarding; ongoing account access needs its own rules.

## Evidence and open questions

- Supplied context: occasional volunteers, shared household email accounts, low-support priority, no accepted approach.
- Unknown: whether forwarded links have caused mistaken enrollment, additional access, or support requests. No examples or measurements were supplied.
- Unknown: what access an invitation grants. Sensitive or privileged access could change the recommendation.
- Support reduction is a hypothesis, not a measured result. External research and service testing were unavailable.

## Next action

The user and coordinators can assess one concrete forwarding scenario: if the wrong household member redeems a link, what access do they receive and what correction is needed? Use that answer to accept the recommendation or require tighter redemption controls. This discussion authorizes no implementation or experiment.
